After a decade of arguing about estimates I've landed somewhere unfashionable: the number matters less than the conversation that produces it.

Teams that estimate badly usually aren't bad at arithmetic. They're missing information, and the estimate is where that shows up. "Three days or three weeks, depending" is not a failure of estimation — it's a discovered dependency, surfaced early, in the only forum where anyone was going to ask.

I stopped chasing accuracy and started paying attention to spread. A wide range means we don't understand the problem yet, and the useful response is a spike, not pressure to narrow the number.

The teams I trust most give me wide ranges early and narrow ones later. The ones I worry about give me confident numbers immediately.
