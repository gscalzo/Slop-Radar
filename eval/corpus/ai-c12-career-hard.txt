I left a job I was good at for one I was bad at, and it was the right call.

The old role had stopped teaching me anything around month eighteen. I was competent, well-reviewed, and quietly bored. The new one had me out of my depth for most of a year — I misjudged timelines, I over-promised, I had to ask questions I felt I should already know the answers to.

What I underestimated was how much of my professional identity was built on being the person who knew things. Giving that up temporarily was harder than any technical part of the job.

Two years on, the range I picked up is the reason I'm employable now, and the bored version of me would have been fine for another decade and then suddenly wouldn't have been.

Competence is comfortable. It's also a plateau.
