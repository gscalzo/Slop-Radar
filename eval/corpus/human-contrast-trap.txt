Something I got wrong for most of my career: I thought code review was about catching bugs.

It isn't about catching bugs — it's about spreading context. The bugs you catch in review are the cheap ones. The expensive ones need a test suite or a type system, not a human skimming a diff on a Friday afternoon.

What review actually buys you is that two people now know why that module exists. When one of them leaves, the knowledge doesn't leave with them.

We changed how we review once we understood that. Fewer comments about style, more questions about intent.
