Something that took me too long to learn about getting promoted.

Most people treat it as an outcome of doing good work. It isn't, quite. It's an outcome of someone senior being able to make an argument on your behalf in a room you aren't in.

Which means your job isn't only to do the work. It's to make the argument easy to construct: what changed because of you, in terms the room already cares about, with numbers if numbers exist and specifics if they don't.

I've watched excellent engineers get passed over because their manager couldn't describe their contribution in a sentence. Not because the contribution was small — because it was diffuse.

Write the sentence yourself. Hand it to your manager. Most of them will be grateful.
