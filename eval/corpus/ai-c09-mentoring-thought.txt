The most transformative mentoring relationship of my career lasted eleven minutes.

I was 26, terrified, and presenting to a room of executives for the first time. Afterwards, a woman I'd never met stopped me in the corridor.

She said: "You apologised four times in twelve minutes. You didn't have anything to apologise for."

That's it. That's the whole mentorship.

I've thought about those eleven minutes for fifteen years. I've thought about them in every negotiation, every board meeting, every difficult conversation.

We overcomplicate mentoring. We build programmes, matching algorithms, quarterly check-ins. Sometimes what someone needs is one accurate observation from someone with nothing to gain.

Be that person for someone this week. It costs you nothing. It might cost them a decade if you don't.
