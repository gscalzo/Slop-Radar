A pattern I've noticed in performance reviews, having now written about ninety of them.

The feedback people remember is almost never the feedback about their weaknesses. It's the feedback that names something they were doing well but couldn't see, and gives it a word.

Someone who keeps meetings on track doesn't know that's a skill until you call it facilitation. Someone who quietly de-risks projects thinks they're being difficult until you call it judgment.

Naming a strength precisely does more for someone's development than cataloguing three areas for improvement, because it tells them what to do more of, and gives them language to describe themselves in the next promotion round.

I still write the improvement sections. I just stopped believing they were the important part.
