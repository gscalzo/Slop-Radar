We spent 14 months building a product nobody wanted.

The signs were there from month two. Customer interviews were lukewarm. The pilot users churned. Our champion inside the design partner left and nobody replaced her.

We kept going. Because we'd told the board. Because we'd hired for it. Because stopping felt like failing.

We shipped in March. We killed it in June.

Here's what I'd tell my past self: the moment you notice you're arguing for a project using reasons about the past — what you've spent, what you've promised, what you've built — you've already stopped making a decision and started defending one.

Sunk cost isn't a bias you read about. It's a feeling. It feels exactly like commitment.

Kill your projects earlier. It's the cheapest thing you'll ever do.
